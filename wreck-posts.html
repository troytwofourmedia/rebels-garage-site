The holiday season brings packed shopping centers, crowded parking lots, and a lot of distracted drivers. It's no surprise that parking lot fender benders pick up this time of year. Even a slow-speed bump can leave you with a cracked bumper, scuffed paint, or damage you can't see right away.

Here's what to do if it happens to you.

1. Stay calm and check for injuries

Most parking lot accidents are minor, but make sure everyone is okay before anything else. If anyone is hurt, call 911.

2. Take photos of everything

Photograph both vehicles, the damage, license plates, and the surrounding area. Wide shots that show where the cars ended up can be just as helpful as close-ups.

3. Exchange information

Get the other driver's name, phone number, insurance company, and policy number. If the other car is unattended, leave a note with your contact information and take a photo of the vehicle and plate.

4. Look for witnesses and cameras

Many shopping centers have security cameras. Ask a store or property manager if footage may be available, and get contact information from anyone who saw what happened.

5. Don't ignore minor damage

A small dent or crack in a bumper cover can hide damage underneath, like broken brackets, bent reinforcements, or damaged parking sensors. Getting it inspected early helps make sure everything is documented and repaired properly.

6. Know you can choose your repair shop

If you're filing an insurance claim, you have the right to choose where your vehicle is repaired. Wreck Center works with all major insurance providers and can help guide you through the claims process.

Today's vehicles are packed with safety technology. Lane departure warnings, blind spot monitoring, automatic emergency braking, adaptive cruise control, and parking sensors all rely on cameras and sensors mounted in your bumpers, mirrors, grille, and windshield.

When a vehicle is in an accident, those systems can be affected even when the damage looks minor.

Where safety sensors are located

Many sensors are mounted in the areas most likely to be damaged in a collision, including front and rear bumpers, side mirrors, the grille area, and behind the windshield. A minor impact can knock a sensor out of position or damage its mounting bracket.

Why sensor position matters

Safety systems depend on sensors pointing exactly where they're supposed to. A sensor that's even slightly out of alignment may not detect objects correctly, which can affect how your safety features respond.

Signs something may be wrong

  • Warning lights on your dashboard after an accident
  • Safety features that turn off or display error messages
  • Parking sensors that beep constantly or not at all
  • Lane keeping or cruise control that behaves differently than before

What to ask your repair shop

When you get a collision estimate, ask how the shop will check your vehicle's safety systems and whether any sensors or cameras need to be inspected, replaced, or recalibrated as part of the repair. Many manufacturers have specific procedures for this work.

It's easy to put off small body damage. A door ding from a parking lot, a scratch from a shopping cart, or a rock chip on the hood doesn't seem urgent. But small damage can turn into bigger problems if it's left alone, especially with the intense sun we get in Southern Nevada.

Exposed metal can lead to rust

When a scratch or chip goes through the paint and clear coat, it can expose bare metal. Over time, that can lead to corrosion that spreads under the surrounding paint.

Sun and heat make damage worse

Las Vegas and Henderson sun is hard on paint. Damaged clear coat can peel, fade, and spread when it's exposed to heat and UV rays day after day.

Small repairs usually cost less

Fixing a scratch or chip early is often simpler than repairing a larger area later. Waiting can mean more prep work, more paint, and more time in the shop.

It protects your vehicle's value

Clean, well-maintained paint makes a big difference when you sell or trade in your vehicle. Dents and scratches can lower what someone is willing to pay.

It happens fast. You clip a curb while parking, hit a pothole you didn't see, or bump a parking block a little too hard. Your car may look fine, but impacts like these can affect your suspension and alignment.

Here are the signs to watch for.

Your vehicle pulls to one side

If your car drifts left or right when you're driving straight, your alignment may be off.

Your steering wheel is off center

A steering wheel that sits crooked when you're driving straight is a common sign of an alignment problem.

Uneven or fast tire wear

Misalignment can cause tires to wear unevenly, shortening their life and costing you money.

Vibration or shaking

Vibration in the steering wheel or seat can point to a bent wheel, a damaged tire, or suspension problems.

Clunking or knocking noises

New noises when going over bumps can mean damaged suspension components.

The ride feels different

If your vehicle feels bouncier, leans more in turns, or just doesn't drive like it used to, have it checked.

Why it matters

Suspension and alignment problems can affect handling, braking, and tire life. Catching them early can help prevent more expensive repairs and keep your vehicle safe to drive.

Your brakes are one of the most important safety systems on your vehicle. With stop-and-go traffic on Boulder Highway, the 215, and busy Henderson streets, they work hard every day. Knowing the warning signs can help you catch problems early.

Squealing or squeaking

Many brake pads have a wear indicator that makes a high-pitched sound when the pads are getting low. If you hear it regularly, it's time for an inspection.

Grinding

A grinding noise can mean the pads are worn down and metal is contacting metal. This can damage rotors quickly, so don't wait.

Vibration when braking

A pulsing brake pedal or a shaking steering wheel when you stop can be a sign of warped or uneven rotors.

A soft or spongy pedal

If your brake pedal feels soft or sinks closer to the floor than usual, there may be a problem with the brake fluid or hydraulic system. Have it checked right away.

Pulling to one side when braking

If your vehicle pulls left or right when you brake, it could be a caliper, hose, or pad issue.

Brake warning light

A brake or ABS warning light on your dashboard is your vehicle telling you something needs attention.

After an accident

Collisions can damage brake lines, wheels, and suspension components connected to your braking system. If you've been in an accident, ask for those systems to be inspected along with the body damage.
