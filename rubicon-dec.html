Winter in Southern Oregon can change fast. One morning it's rain in Grants Pass, and by the time you reach the Siskiyou Pass or head over to the coast, you're dealing with snow, ice, or chain requirements. A little preparation now can keep you safe and save you from an expensive surprise later.

1. Check your tires before the weather turns

Your tires are the only thing touching the road. Check tread depth, look for cracks or bulges, and make sure the pressure is set to your vehicle's recommendation. Cold temperatures lower tire pressure, so check it more often in winter.

2. Consider a dedicated set of winter wheels

If you run winter or studded tires, mounting them on a second set of wheels makes the seasonal swap faster and cheaper. You avoid paying to have tires dismounted and remounted twice a year. A set of used OEM wheels is an affordable way to do it, and they fit your vehicle the way the factory intended.

3. Carry chains and practice putting them on

Chain requirements can go up quickly on mountain passes. Keep chains or approved traction devices in your vehicle, and practice installing them in your driveway before you need them on the side of the road.

4. Check road conditions before you leave

Before heading over a pass, check ODOT's TripCheck for current conditions, closures, and chain requirements. A few minutes of planning can save you hours of waiting.

5. Test your battery

Cold weather is hard on batteries. If your engine cranks slowly or your battery is more than a few years old, have it tested before winter sets in.

6. Make sure you can see and be seen

Replace worn wiper blades, top off your washer fluid with a winter blend, and check that all of your lights work. A cloudy or cracked headlight can seriously cut your visibility on dark winter roads. A used OEM headlight assembly is often a budget-friendly fix.

7. Keep an emergency kit in your vehicle

Pack a blanket, gloves, a flashlight, water, snacks, an ice scraper, jumper cables, and a phone charger. If you get stuck waiting for a road to clear, you'll be glad you did.

1. Are used auto parts reliable?

A quality used OEM part was built by your vehicle's original manufacturer and designed for your vehicle. Condition matters, so buy from a supplier who inspects and accurately describes what they sell.

2. What's the difference between OEM and aftermarket?

OEM parts are made by or for the original vehicle manufacturer. Aftermarket parts are made by other companies. Quality varies across aftermarket brands, while OEM parts match the original specifications.

3. How much money can I save?

It depends on the part and vehicle, but used OEM parts often cost significantly less than buying the same part new from a dealer.

Start with your VIN. Year, make, and model are helpful, but trim level, engine, drivetrain, and options can all affect fit. Matching part numbers is another great way to confirm.

5. Do used parts come with a warranty?

Warranty coverage varies by supplier and part. Always ask what's covered and for how long before you buy.

6. Can I return a part if it doesn't fit?

Return policies vary, so ask before you order. Confirming fitment up front is the best way to avoid a return in the first place.

7. Will my mechanic install a used part?

Replacing a damaged wheel, building a winter set, or upgrading the look of your vehicle? Getting the right wheel comes down to a few key measurements. Here's what they mean and how to find them.

Wheel size

Wheel size is written as diameter by width, like 18x8. The first number is the diameter in inches, and the second is the width. Your tires are built for a specific wheel diameter, so this number needs to match.

Bolt pattern

The bolt pattern tells you how many lug holes the wheel has and the diameter of the circle they form, like 5x114.3. If the bolt pattern doesn't match your vehicle, the wheel won't mount.

Offset

Offset is the distance between the wheel's mounting surface and its centerline, measured in millimeters. It controls how far in or out the wheel sits in the wheel well. The wrong offset can cause rubbing on the suspension or fenders.

Center bore

The center bore is the hole in the middle of the wheel that fits over your vehicle's hub. OEM wheels are made with the correct center bore for the vehicles they came on, which helps the wheel sit centered and reduces vibration.

Where to find this information

Many wheels have specs stamped on the back of a spoke or inside the barrel. You can also find your vehicle's factory wheel specs on the driver's door jamb sticker or in your owner's manual. When in doubt, your VIN and a photo of your current wheel go a long way.

Why OEM wheels make it easier

When you choose an OEM wheel originally made for your vehicle, the size, bolt pattern, offset, and center bore are already designed to work together. That takes a lot of the guesswork out of the process. It also makes it easy to replace a single damaged wheel and keep a matching set.
