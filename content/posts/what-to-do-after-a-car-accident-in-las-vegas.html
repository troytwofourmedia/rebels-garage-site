Nobody plans for a fender bender on Flamingo or a rear-end hit on the 95. When it happens, a few calm steps make the rest of the process much easier, from the insurance claim to the repair. Here is the checklist we give our own friends and family.

1. Make sure everyone is safe

Check yourself and your passengers first. If anyone is hurt, call 911. If the vehicles can move and no one is injured, get them out of traffic lanes and turn on your hazards.

2. Document everything

Your phone is your best tool here. Take photos of both vehicles from several angles, close ups of the damage, license plates, the street and any skid marks or debris. Get the other driver's name, phone, insurance company and policy number. If there are witnesses, ask for their contact info too.

3. Report it

For crashes with injuries or significant damage, file a police report. Then call your insurance company to open a claim. Have your photos and the other driver's information ready.

4. Look for hidden damage

A bumper cover can look fine while the reinforcement bar, brackets or sensors behind it are cracked or bent. Watch for these signs after even a minor collision:

  • Panels, doors or the hood that no longer line up
  • New rattles, pulling to one side or uneven tire wear
  • Warning lights on the dash, especially for parking sensors or driver assist systems
  • Fluid under the vehicle where there was none before

5. Get a professional estimate

A body shop estimate tells you what really needs to be repaired, not just what is visible. At The Rebels Garage we inspect the damage, explain what we found and give you a written estimate before any work begins.

6. Ask questions before you approve the repair

A good shop will be happy to answer them. Ask what parts will be used, how long the repair should take, how paint will be matched and how you will be updated along the way.
