When a repair is done right, you should not be able to tell which panel was fixed. That comes down to color matching, and it is harder than most people think.

Why "the same color" isn't always the same

Every vehicle has a paint code, but that code is only a starting point. Factory paint can vary slightly from batch to batch, and the color on your car has been aging in the sun since the day it left the factory. In Las Vegas, that aging happens faster than most places.

How a match is made

  • Start with the code. The paint code gives us the base formula.
  • Check the real color. We compare against your actual vehicle, not just the code, and adjust the formula as needed.
  • Spray test cards. Test panels are compared to the car in different light before paint goes on the repair.
  • Blend into neighboring panels. Color is feathered into the surrounding panels so your eye cannot find an edge.
  • Clear coat and finish. The final clear coat is applied and finished to match the gloss of the rest of the vehicle.

Metallics and pearls are the hardest

Metallic and pearl paints have tiny flakes that reflect light. How they are sprayed changes how they lay down, which changes how the color looks from different angles. These finishes take extra skill to match.
