A dent is one of the most common reasons people visit a body shop, and one of the most common questions we hear is whether the car needs paint. The answer depends on the dent. Here is how we decide.

What is paintless dent repair?

Paintless dent repair, or PDR, is a technique where a technician works the metal back into shape from behind the panel using specialized tools. Because the factory paint stays in place, there is no filler, no sanding and no refinishing. When it is the right fit, it is usually faster and less expensive.

When PDR works best

  • Door dings from parking lots
  • Hail damage
  • Shallow, rounded dents where the paint is not cracked or chipped
  • Dents that are not right on a sharp edge of the panel

When you need traditional dent repair

If the paint is cracked, chipped or scratched through, PDR cannot fix the finish. Deep creases, dents on sharp body lines or panel edges, and stretched metal usually need traditional repair: the panel is straightened, smoothed, primed and refinished, then the paint is blended into the surrounding panels so the repair disappears.

Why you shouldn't ignore damaged paint

Las Vegas sun is brutal on paint. Once the clear coat or paint is broken, UV and moisture can work under the edges and lead to peeling or rust over time. A small chip around a dent is worth fixing before it spreads.

How to tell which one you need

Look closely at the dent in good light. If the paint looks smooth and unbroken, there is a good chance PDR will work. If you can see cracks, chips or bare metal, plan on a traditional repair. The fastest way to know for sure is to have it inspected.
