Living in the desert has its perks, but it is tough on your vehicle's finish. Months of triple digit heat and intense UV can turn a glossy paint job dull, chalky or peeling. Here is what is happening and what you can do about it.

What the sun does to paint

Modern paint has a base color coat protected by a clear coat. UV exposure slowly breaks down that clear coat. First it loses its shine, then it can turn hazy or chalky, and eventually it can crack and peel. Red, black and other dark colors tend to show it first.

Heat, dust and sprinklers

Hot panels make bird droppings, tree sap and bug splatter bake into the finish faster. Fine desert dust acts like sandpaper when it is wiped off dry. And hard water spots from sprinklers can etch the surface if they sit in the sun.

How to protect your finish

  • Park in a garage or under cover whenever you can
  • Wash regularly, and never wipe dust off a dry car
  • Clean droppings and sap off quickly
  • Use a quality wax, sealant or ceramic coating to add a layer of UV protection
  • Use a windshield sunshade to help protect the dash too

When the clear coat is already failing

Once clear coat is peeling, polishing will not bring it back. The damaged area needs to be sanded and refinished. The good news is that this can often be done panel by panel, with the new paint matched to your factory color.
